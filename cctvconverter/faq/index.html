Formats & conversion

Yes. Most Chinese DVRs (Xiongmai, HiSilicon and white-label rebrands) wrap MPEG‐PS in a proprietary header starting with the bytes IMKH. CCTV Converter detects that signature and converts to MP4 with no quality loss in seconds.

Yes. The default conversion is a stream copy, meaning the video and audio packets are repackaged into MP4 without being decoded or re-encoded. The picture quality is the same as the source. Re-encoding only happens as a fallback if the original stream is incompatible with MP4, and it uses high-quality H.264.

Yes — tick “Add timestamp overlay to converted video” in the Convert options menu before you convert. A date/time clock taken from each file’s recording time is burned into the bottom-left of the MP4, incrementing per frame. (See our Terms on relying on output as a sole legal record.)

Raw .264 and .265 files don't always store how fast they were recorded. CCTV Converter works it out from the recording times in the file names where it can. If a file still plays at the wrong speed, open its ⋯ menu, set the camera's frame rate under Recorded at, and the app offers to convert it again.

Editing, viewing & sharing

Yes. Select a file and click Edit… under the preview. The built-in editor lets you trim to the exact frame, crop, rotate, and adjust brightness, contrast, saturation, sharpness and gamma, apply one-tap look presets, reduce night-time grain, and set the output resolution and quality. You can save your settings as presets, automatically blur, pixelate or black out faces, and draw a box over any area — a number plate, a person, an object — that the app then tracks for you. Edits always export to a new MP4 — your original files are never modified.

Yes — tick the files and choose Multi View from the Tools menu. The Multi-Camera view plays up to six recordings side by side on a single timeline, with per-camera offset nudging so you can line up clocks that have drifted apart. Snapshot every angle at once, build a contact sheet, or export a synchronized composite video.

Tick your footage and choose Extract motion highlights… from the Tools menu (or a day's ⋯ menu). The app scans for movement, then exports just the clips where something happens — with adjustable sensitivity and pre/post-roll padding so you keep the run-up and run-out of each event. Save each event separately or as one combined highlight reel, and skip hours of an empty driveway.

Open the clip in the editor and choose Evidence package from the Export menu. You get one folder with a checked copy of the original recording, the MP4 as edited, a report listing what was changed, SHA-256 checksums and a README. If you only need the details and checksums, Evidence report creates a page that opens in any browser and can be printed to PDF. (See our Terms on relying on output as a sole legal record.)

Yes. In the editor, choose For phone / WhatsApp from the Export menu to save a small copy (16 MB or less) that plays on phones and can be sent in a chat. Compress for email makes a copy of 25 MB or less.

Performance, languages & requirements

Usually many times faster than realtime for a typical CCTV bitrate: a 30-second clip often converts in under a second. With parallel conversion enabled (up to 16 files at once), a full day of recordings — a few hundred clips — can take under a minute on a typical laptop. Optional GPU acceleration (NVIDIA / Intel / AMD) speeds up re-encodes. You can stop at any time; converting again picks up the files that aren't done yet.

16 languages: English, Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia), Vietnamese (Tiếng Việt), Thai, Filipino, Chinese (Simplified), Japanese, Korean, Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil), French, German, Russian, Turkish, Ukrainian and Arabic (right-to-left). The app auto-detects your Windows language on first launch and you can switch any time from the globe icon in the toolbar.

Yes. CCTV Converter does its work entirely on your machine and never uploads your footage. Windows 10 or Windows 11, x64. The Microsoft Store version is self-contained — it bundles the .NET runtime and ffmpeg, so there are no prerequisites to install and no admin rights are needed for normal use.
